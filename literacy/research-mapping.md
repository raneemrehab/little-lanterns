# Learning Lanterns · Research-to-design map and design notes

**The source tracker contains no research references.** No citations were added from memory. "Design rationale" below is reasoning, not evidence. These games give practice; none is claimed to produce measurable improvement.

## Shared design system

- Feedback: Nothing is ever marked wrong. Retry lines are warm, and after two guesses the game makes the task easier or does it together with the child. Every choice stays tappable.
- Layouts: Desktop and tablet use a 16:10 landscape stage, scaled to fit, like the existing games. Phones in portrait reflow to a stacked layout: the guide and prompt at the top, the play area in the middle and choices in a bottom row within thumb reach.
- Accessibility in every game:
  - Every spoken prompt has a speaker button to replay it and an optional caption
  - Tap targets at least 64 px at tablet size; nothing depends on colour alone
  - No time limits unless the skill needs one, and those are adjustable
  - Reduced-motion and reduced-distraction modes (plain background, fewer decorations)
  - Break button on every screen; sessions end after 5 rounds at a natural stopping point
  - Keyboard and switch access: tab through choices, Enter to choose

## 1. Echo Cave

Literacy · Unit 1 · Spark–Flame (2–5) · Guide: Pip the bat

> Approved earlier and already built. Shown here so the set reads as one collection; design unchanged.

- **Primary objective:** Hear when two words rhyme, and hear the beats (syllables) in a word.
- **Supporting skills:** Rhyme recognition; Rhyme odd-one-out; Syllable clapping and counting
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Auditory discrimination; listening attention
- **1. What the child does:** Rhyme Stones: Pip says a word, the child taps the stone whose picture rhymes. Beat Drum: the child taps the drum once for every beat in a word.
- **2. What it practises:** Listening to the ends of words (rhyme) and splitting words into beats (syllables), with pictures only and no letters.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Spark (2–3): Rhyme from 2 very different pictures; tap along as Pip claps
  - 2 · Glow (3–4): 3 pictures; tap 1–3 beats alone
  - 3 · Flame (4–5): 4 pictures with a near-rhyme (hat / hut); words up to 4 beats
  - 4 · Flame (4–5): Odd one out; say how many beats by tapping stones
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: the two words echo ("hat… cat") then a praise line. First miss: "Good guess! Let's listen again." Second miss: one choice is removed. Third: Pip shows the answer.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Picture-only rhyme and beat work suits children who are not yet reading; one variable changes per level.
- **6. Beyond the game:** Rhyme stones and a hand drum in the physical set; clapping names and nursery rhymes at home.
- **Visual elements:** Cave with glowing stones; Pip the bat; 43 rhyme pictures and 13 beat pictures, no letters; Hand drum; Glow-up cave on completion
- **Accessibility:** Every picture says its name when tapped; Captions match Pip's words exactly; Echo effect can be switched off

## 2. Sound Safari

Literacy · Unit 2 · Glow–Flame (3–5) · Guide: Ziggy the zebra

- **Primary objective:** Hear the first sound of a spoken word and find another word that starts with the same sound.
- **Supporting skills:** Initial phoneme isolation; Same / different first sound; Sorting by first sound
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Auditory discrimination; selective attention
- **1. What the child does:** Ziggy looks through binoculars and says a word ("sun… /s/"). The child taps the animal or object in the grass that starts with the same sound.
- **2. What it practises:** Isolating the first phoneme of a word and comparing it with other words, by ear only. Pictures have no letters.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Glow (3–4): Same or different? Two pictures, tap the thumbs-up if they start the same
  - 2 · Glow (3–4): Find the match from 3 pictures with very different first sounds (s, m, b)
  - 3 · Flame (4–5): 4 pictures, first sounds closer together (s / sh, f / th)
  - 4 · Flame (4–5): Sort 6 pictures into two baskets by first sound
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: Ziggy repeats both words stretching the first sound ("sss-un, sss-ock") and the picture hops into the jeep. First miss: "Good guess, but try again". Second: Ziggy stretches the first sound of each choice. Third: "Let's do it together" and the match glows.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Builds on Echo Cave's whole-word listening by narrowing attention to the first sound; still no letters, so the step is listening only.
- **6. Beyond the game:** I-spy with first sounds on walks; sorting toy animals by first sound.
- **Visual elements:** Savanna with tall grass; Ziggy with binoculars; Picture cards in grass tufts; Safari jeep that fills with found pictures; Two baskets (Level 4)
- **Accessibility:** Continuous sounds (s, m, f) used first because they can be stretched; Every picture says its name on tap; Binocular view can be turned off in reduced-distraction mode

## 3. Letter Post Office

Literacy · Unit 3 · Glow–Flame (3–5) · Guide: Penny the pigeon

- **Primary objective:** Link a letter shape to its sound: hear a sound and find its letter, see a letter and say its sound.
- **Supporting skills:** Letter-sound correspondence (lower case first); Telling similar letters apart; Recall of taught letters
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Visual discrimination; recall
- **1. What the child does:** Penny brings a parcel with a picture on it. The child hears its first sound and posts it through the slot with the matching letter.
- **2. What it practises:** Matching a sound to its written letter. Letters are introduced in small sets (s a t p i n first) and reviewed from earlier sets.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Glow (3–4): 2 slots, letters from s a t p i n, picture plus sound
  - 2 · Glow (3–4): 3 slots, adds m d g o c k
  - 3 · Flame (4–5): Sound only (no picture), 3 slots; similar shapes (b / d, n / m) appear
  - 4 · Flame (4–5): Reverse: a letter lights up, child taps the picture that starts with that sound
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: the parcel slides in, the slot lights and Penny says the sound and the word ("/s/… sun"). First miss: "Good guess, but try again". Second: the right slot's letter is traced in light while the sound plays. Third: Penny posts it together with the child.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Sound-first, then letter: the child already isolates first sounds (Sound Safari), so this game adds the letter only.
- **6. Beyond the game:** Magnetic letters on the fridge; finding the first letter of their own name on signs.
- **Visual elements:** Post office counter; Penny with satchel; Parcels with pictures; Postbox slots with large lower-case letters; Stamp book that fills
- **Accessibility:** Letters in a clear school font with single-storey a and g; Each slot plays its sound on tap; Slots differ by letter only, never by colour

## 4. Robot Talk

Literacy · Unit 4 · Flame–Beacon (4–6) · Guide: Bolt the robot

- **Primary objective:** Blend separate spoken sounds into a word (c… a… t → cat).
- **Supporting skills:** Oral blending of onset and rime; Blending 3 and then 4 phonemes; Blending with letters shown (Beacon)
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Sequencing; working memory (load kept low)
- **1. What the child does:** Bolt can only talk in separate sounds. A light flashes for each sound. The child pushes the sounds together and taps the picture of the word.
- **2. What it practises:** Holding a short run of sounds in mind and blending them into a word. Each sound has its own light, so the child can replay one sound without starting again.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Flame (4–5): Two parts: c… at; 2 pictures
  - 2 · Flame (4–5): Three sounds: c… a… t; 3 pictures, one shares the first sound
  - 3 · Beacon (5–6): Three sounds, close choices (cat / cap / cot)
  - 4 · Beacon (5–6): Letters appear under the lights; four-sound words (f… r… o… g)
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: the lights slide together into one bar and Bolt says the whole word smoothly. First miss: "Good guess, but try again". Second: Bolt says the sounds more slowly and closer together. Third: lights merge on their own.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Load kept low by design: at most 4 sounds, each replayable, no time limit. Bridges Echo Cave (whole words) and Potion Spells (splitting).
- **6. Beyond the game:** Robot-talk games at home: "Can you touch your h… a… t?"
- **Visual elements:** Workshop; Bolt with a chest screen of sound lights; Picture choices on gears; Light bar that merges; Bolt's battery fills with each word
- **Accessibility:** Each light replays its own sound; No time pressure; Picture choices never rely on colour

## 5. Potion Spells

Literacy · Unit 5 · Flame–Beacon (4–6) · Guide: Fenn the fox

- **Primary objective:** Split a spoken word into its sounds and spell it with letters, one sound per box.
- **Supporting skills:** Phoneme segmentation; Sound-to-letter spelling of CVC words; Digraphs (sh, ch, th); Re-reading to check
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Sequencing; checking own work
- **1. What the child does:** Fenn needs a word for each spell. The child taps a bubble for each sound they hear, then drops a letter bottle into each sound box. The potion only fizzes when the child taps "Check my spell".
- **2. What it practises:** Segmenting words into phonemes and choosing the letter for each one, then re-reading the spelling to check it.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Flame (4–5): Sound bubbles only: tap one bubble per sound (oral)
  - 2 · Flame (4–5): Spell CVC words with 4 letter bottles
  - 3 · Beacon (5–6): 6 letter bottles, words with sh / ch / th
  - 4 · Beacon (5–6): Spot the slip: a spell has one wrong letter; find and swap it
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: "Check my spell" reads the word back sound by sound, then the cauldron fizzes into a colour. Not yet: the check reads what the child built ("d-o-b") and the box that differs glows softly so the child can hear and fix it. No box is ever marked wrong.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Checking is built into the action (the child taps Check and hears their own spelling read back), which supports the 'checking own work' cognitive target.
- **6. Beyond the game:** Spelling words with magnetic letters on a 3-box mat; sounding out shopping list words.
- **Visual elements:** Magic kitchen; Fenn with spoon; Cauldron; Elkonin sound boxes; Letter bottles; Spell book of potions made
- **Accessibility:** Drag or tap-then-tap; Sound boxes are large and numbered by position; Read-back speed adjustable

## 6. Word Train

Literacy · Unit 6 · Beacon (5–7) · Guide: Otto the otter

- **Primary objective:** Decode short words and simple sentences, then check that what was read makes sense.
- **Supporting skills:** Decoding CVC and CCVC words; Reading common exception words; Sentence reading; Monitoring meaning
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Stop-and-think (not guessing); checking meaning
- **1. What the child does:** Each engine carries a word or sentence. The child reads it (sound buttons under the word can help) and couples the carriage with the matching picture. At sentence level, some sentences are silly and go to the "silly shed".
- **2. What it practises:** Decoding by sounding out and blending, and checking meaning instead of guessing from the picture. Pictures appear only after the child has had time to read.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Beacon (5–6): CVC words, sound buttons on; 3 pictures
  - 2 · Beacon (5–6): CCVC / CVCC words and 2 exception words (the, I)
  - 3 · Beacon (6–7): Short sentences: 'The pig is in the mud.'
  - 4 · Beacon (6–7): Sense or silly? 'The cup can run.' goes to the silly shed
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: the carriage couples and the train toots, then the word is read aloud. First miss: "Good guess, but try again" and sound buttons pulse. Second: the word is sounded out slowly. Pictures arrive after a short 'read first' pause so the child reads instead of guessing.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: The read-first pause and silly-sentence check target the source's 'stop-and-think (not guessing)' goal.
- **6. Beyond the game:** Reading labels and signs aloud; 'sense or silly' sentence games with an adult.
- **Visual elements:** Railway through hills; Otto in the cab; Word engine; Picture carriages; Silly shed; Station board of words read
- **Accessibility:** Sound buttons under every grapheme; Font size adjustable; dyslexia-friendly spacing option; Read-first pause adjustable by adults

## 7. Chunk Kitchen

Literacy · Unit 7 · Beacon–Lighthouse (6–9) · Guide: Bea the bear

- **Primary objective:** Read and build words from larger chunks: rimes (-ain, -ight), then prefixes and suffixes (un-, -ing, -ed).
- **Supporting skills:** Word families (onset + rime); Vowel teams; Prefixes and suffixes; Switching a chunk to make a new word
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Pattern recognition; flexibility
- **1. What the child does:** Bea is cooking words. A chunk sits in the pot ("ain"). The child picks the starter from the spice jars to make the word on the order ticket ("train"). Later, they add endings and beginnings.
- **2. What it practises:** Seeing a known chunk inside a word and swapping parts flexibly, instead of sounding every letter.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Beacon (6–7): Rime families: -at, -in, -ock; pick the onset
  - 2 · Beacon (6–7): Vowel-team chunks: -ain, -ight, -oat; blends as onsets (tr, fl)
  - 3 · Lighthouse (7–8): Add an ending: -ing, -ed, -s; read the new word
  - 4 · Lighthouse (8–9): Switch it: change one chunk to make the order word (un-lock / re-lock)
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: the chunks slide together, the dish is served and the word is read with the chunks highlighted. Not yet: Bea reads the word the child made ("rain!" when "train" was ordered) so the child hears the difference; a real word made is praised as a real word.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Swapping one chunk at a time targets pattern recognition and flexibility named in the source.
- **6. Beyond the game:** Word-family lists on the fridge; spotting -ing and -ed in books.
- **Visual elements:** Bakery kitchen; Bea in an apron; Pot with chunk; Spice-jar onsets; Order tickets; Menu board of words cooked
- **Accessibility:** Chunks colour-banded and labelled; colour never the only cue; Order ticket can be read aloud; Drag or tap-then-tap

## 8. Word Collector

Literacy · Unit 8 · Glow–Lighthouse (3–9) · Guide: Rosie the robin

- **Primary objective:** Learn and remember rich 'story words' (enormous, soggy, peek) by meeting them in a story, picturing them and using them again.
- **Supporting skills:** Vocabulary in context; Word meaning by picture choice; Opposites and similar words (later levels); Recall days later
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Language comprehension; long-term recall
- **1. What the child does:** Rosie reads a short story page and stops at a story word. The child taps the picture that shows it, and the word goes into their word jar. Older children sort collected words by meaning.
- **2. What it practises:** Hearing a new word in context, linking it to a picture, and recalling it later. Words come back in later sessions so recall is practised over time.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Glow (3–4): Everyday describing words (big, wet); 2 pictures
  - 2 · Flame (4–5): Story words (enormous, tiny, soggy); 3 pictures
  - 3 · Beacon (5–7): Action and feeling words (tiptoe, grumpy); word comes back from a previous day
  - 4 · Lighthouse (7–9): Shades of meaning: put warm, hot, boiling in order; find the opposite
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: the picture flies into the jar and Rosie uses the word in a new sentence. First miss: "Good guess, but try again" and Rosie re-reads the sentence. Second: Rosie acts the word out with a short animation.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Words recur across sessions to support the 'long-term recall' cognitive skill; each word is heard in a sentence before it is tested.
- **6. Beyond the game:** A 'word of the day' jar at home; using collected words when talking about pictures.
- **Visual elements:** Woodland library; Rosie on a book; Story page with highlighted word; Picture choices; Word jars on a shelf
- **Accessibility:** Story text read aloud with word highlighting; Collected words can be heard again any time; Picture choices are clearly different at early levels

## 9. Puppet Theatre

Literacy · Unit 9 · Beacon–Lighthouse (6–9) · Guide: Mabel the mouse

- **Primary objective:** Read a short script smoothly, in phrases, with expression.
- **Supporting skills:** Phrasing (reading in meaningful chunks); Accuracy and pace; Expression; Understanding what was read
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Sustained attention; checking own work
- **1. What the child does:** The child is a puppet in Mabel's play. They hear the line read well (Listen), read it together with the highlight (Together), then read it alone (My turn) while the phrase highlight moves at their chosen pace. A quick question checks they followed the scene.
- **2. What it practises:** Reading the same short text several times in phrases, the 'listen, together, alone' sequence, then checking understanding.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Beacon (6–7): One-line parts, phrase highlight on, slow pace
  - 2 · Beacon (6–7): Two-line parts with a question mark or exclamation (expression)
  - 3 · Lighthouse (7–8): Short scene, 3 parts, child picks their pace
  - 4 · Lighthouse (8–9): Highlight off for 'My turn'; child taps each phrase as they finish it
- **4. How feedback teaches:** After 'My turn' the audience claps for every finished part; there is no score for reading aloud. The check question uses the usual "Good guess, but try again" and re-plays the line.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Repeated, supported reading of the same text in phrases. The game does not judge reading aloud (no microphone scoring), so it practises fluency without claiming to measure it.
- **6. Beyond the game:** Reading a part in a family play; reading a favourite page several times with expression.
- **Visual elements:** Theatre stage with curtains; Puppets; Script card with phrase highlight; Listen / Together / My turn buttons; Audience that claps
- **Accessibility:** Pace slider for the highlight; Optional recording for a grown-up to listen back (off by default); Large text, wide line spacing

## 10. Story Map Explorer

Literacy · Unit 10 · Glow–Lighthouse (3–9) · Guide: Scout the squirrel

- **Primary objective:** Follow a story: who is in it, where it happens, what happens first, next and last, and why.
- **Supporting skills:** Story structure (beginning, middle, end); Retelling in order; Who / where / what questions; Simple inference (why)
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Sequencing; memory; reasoning
- **1. What the child does:** Scout hears a short story, then walks a map path with three stops. The child places the right picture card at each stop and answers a question at the end.
- **2. What it practises:** Recalling and ordering story events, and answering literal then 'why' questions.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Glow (3–4): Who is in the story? 2 pictures
  - 2 · Flame (4–5): First and last: 2 stops
  - 3 · Beacon (5–7): Beginning, middle, end: 3 stops; where questions
  - 4 · Lighthouse (7–9): Why did it happen? Picture answers that need a small inference
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: Scout walks to the next stop and retells that part. First miss: "Good guess, but try again" and that part of the story replays. Second: two cards remain.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Moves from literal recall to inference across levels; story can be replayed at any time.
- **6. Beyond the game:** Retelling a bedtime story with 'first, next, last'; drawing a story map.
- **Visual elements:** Map with winding path; Scout with a backpack; Three numbered stops; Story picture cards; Treasure chest at the end
- **Accessibility:** Story can be replayed by part; Cards are large pictures, no reading needed until Lighthouse; Path stops are numbered and labelled

## 11. Sky Writer

Literacy · Unit 11 · Flame–Beacon (4–7) · Guide: Juniper the swallow

- **Primary objective:** Form lower-case letters with the right start point and stroke direction.
- **Supporting skills:** Start point and direction; Letter families (c-shapes: c a d g o q); Letter size and position on a line; Writing from memory
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Visual-motor integration; fine motor
- **1. What the child does:** Juniper flies the letter; the child follows the cloud trail with a finger or stylus from the green start dot along the arrows. Later, the guide fades and the child writes from memory on the sky line.
- **2. What it practises:** Correct letter formation as a movement, grouped by families that share a stroke, while saying the letter's sound.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Flame (4–5): Big letter, full guide, arrows and start dot
  - 2 · Flame (4–5): Guide fades to dots; same family
  - 3 · Beacon (5–6): Start dot only; write on a line
  - 4 · Beacon (6–7): Hear a sound, write its letter from memory
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: the trail turns into a rainbow and the letter's sound plays. If the trail leaves the path, the line simply stops and the start dot glows again with "Let's start at the green dot." No line is marked wrong.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Start dot and arrow cues target formation habits; the guide fades step by step.
- **6. Beyond the game:** Writing letters in sand, with a wet brush on paving, then with a pencil.
- **Visual elements:** Sky with clouds; Juniper with a cloud trail; Large letter with start dot and numbered arrows; Rainbow trail on success; Sky line for writing
- **Accessibility:** Works with finger, stylus or mouse; Path tolerance adjustable; Left-handed guide option; Sound plays with every letter

## 12. Postcard Studio

Literacy · Unit 12 · Beacon–Lighthouse (5–9) · Guide: Penny the pigeon

- **Primary objective:** Plan and build a sentence that matches a picture, with a capital letter, spaces and a full stop, then read it back to check.
- **Supporting skills:** Saying the sentence before writing; Word order; Capital letters and full stops; Re-reading to check
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Planning; working memory; checking own work
- **1. What the child does:** Penny needs a postcard. The child says what they see (a 'say it first' button replays their plan as tiles), builds the sentence from word tiles, then taps 'Read it back'. Older children type one missing word themselves.
- **2. What it practises:** Holding a sentence in mind, building it word by word, and checking it makes sense and is punctuated.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Beacon (5–6): 3-word sentences, all tiles given, full stop pre-placed
  - 2 · Beacon (6–7): 4–5 words; add the capital and full stop
  - 3 · Lighthouse (7–8): One distractor tile that doesn't make sense; 'and' joins two ideas
  - 4 · Lighthouse (8–9): Type the key word; choose a better word ('big' or 'huge')
- **4. How feedback teaches:** 'Read it back' reads exactly what is on the card. If it matches the plan and has its capital and full stop, the stamp lands. If not, the read-back makes the gap audible and the missing part (capital, full stop or word) pulses.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: The say-it-first plan and read-back check give a planning and checking routine without relying on handwriting.
- **6. Beyond the game:** Writing a real postcard or a sentence about a weekend photo with an adult.
- **Visual elements:** Studio desk; Postcard with picture; Word tiles; Capital and full-stop tiles; Stamp and postbox on completion
- **Accessibility:** Tiles read aloud on tap; On-screen keyboard with large keys for Level 4; Read-back speed adjustable

## 13. Lantern Lookout

Main · Looking & Listening · Glow–Lighthouse (3–9) · Guide: Luna the owl

- **Primary objective:** Scan a busy scene and find every item that exactly matches the target, ignoring look-alikes.
- **Supporting skills:** Systematic scanning; Fine visual differences; Letter look-alikes (b d p q) at higher levels
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Selective attention
- **1. What the child does:** Luna holds up a target lantern. The child finds and taps every matching lantern in the night sky. The counter shows how many are left.
- **2. What it practises:** Searching left to right, noticing small differences in shape or orientation, and ignoring distractors.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Glow (3–4): Find 3 star lanterns among circles
  - 2 · Flame (4–5): Shapes that differ in one detail (5-point vs 6-point star)
  - 3 · Beacon (5–7): Letter lanterns: find every b among d and p
  - 4 · Lighthouse (7–9): Two targets at once; more lanterns
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: the lantern lights up warm and floats to Luna's string. A look-alike: it gently turns to show its difference next to the target, with "Look closely. Is it the same?"
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Showing the look-alike beside the target makes the difference explicit instead of only saying 'try again'.
- **6. Beyond the game:** Spot-the-difference books; finding a letter on a page of print.
- **Visual elements:** Night sky; Luna on a branch; Target lantern card; Many floating lanterns; String of found lanterns
- **Accessibility:** Count of remaining targets always visible; Reduced-distraction mode lowers the number of lanterns; Tap targets at least 64 px

## 14. Night Sounds

Main · Looking & Listening · Glow–Lighthouse (3–9) · Guide: Bramble the badger

- **Primary objective:** Listen through a stream of night sounds and respond only to the target sound.
- **Supporting skills:** Sustained listening; Holding back a tap for non-targets; Telling similar sounds apart
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Auditory attention; stop-and-think
- **1. What the child does:** Bramble asks the child to tap the big ear only when they hear the owl. Other night sounds (frog, cricket, wind) play in between.
- **2. What it practises:** Keeping attention on sound over time and holding back a response until the target comes.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Glow (3–4): Owl among very different sounds, slow
  - 2 · Flame (4–5): Similar animal sounds (owl, dove)
  - 3 · Beacon (5–7): Two targets: owl and bell
  - 4 · Lighthouse (7–9): Switch: tap for owl, but NOT if the bell rings first
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: the owl's picture appears in the tree. A tap for another sound shows that animal with "That was the frog. Wait for the owl." Nothing is counted against the child.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Showing the sound's source after any tap helps the child match sound to animal.
- **6. Beyond the game:** Listening walks: 'tap your knee when you hear a bird'.
- **Visual elements:** Moonlit wood; Bramble with lantern; Big ear button; Silhouette animals that appear; Moon that fills
- **Accessibility:** Visual sound-wave cue optional (off for listening practice, on for hard-of-hearing children); Sound speed adjustable; Captions name each sound after it plays

## 15. Firefly Deliveries

Main · Thinking Control · Glow–Lighthouse (3–9) · Guide: Flick the firefly

- **Primary objective:** Remember a short route and repeat it in order.
- **Supporting skills:** Remembering a sequence; Order (first, then); Planning a route
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Working memory; planning
- **1. What the child does:** Flick lights up houses one after another. The child taps the houses in the same order to deliver the light.
- **2. What it practises:** Holding a sequence in mind and reproducing it. Length grows by one only after success.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Glow (3–4): 2 houses, slow
  - 2 · Flame (4–5): 3 houses
  - 3 · Beacon (5–7): 4 houses
  - 4 · Lighthouse (7–9): Backwards: deliver in reverse order
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: each house lights as tapped and the chain completes with a glow trail. Not yet: Flick shows the route again, slower, and the length drops by one after two tries.
- **5. Research:** none in the source. Design rationale: Adaptive length (one more after success, one fewer after two tries) keeps the task within reach.
- **6. Beyond the game:** Remembering a two-step errand; 'I went to the shop and bought…' games.
- **Visual elements:** Night village; Flick; Houses with windows; Glow trail; Delivery tally
- **Accessibility:** Houses differ by shape and colour; Each house plays a tone (can be off); Speed adjustable

## 16. Moon Rules

Main · Thinking Control · Glow–Lighthouse (3–9) · Guide: Nova the cat

> The tracker lists an early mockup; it was not found in the project files, so this is a full new design.

- **Primary objective:** Sort cards by one rule, then switch when the moon shows a new rule.
- **Supporting skills:** Following a sorting rule; Switching rules on a cue; Ignoring the old rule
- **Cognitive skills (source):** Flexibility; stop-and-think
- **1. What the child does:** The moon shows the rule (colour or shape). The child drags each card into the basket that matches by that rule. When the moon changes, the rule changes.
- **2. What it practises:** Using a rule, then stopping the old habit and using the new rule.
- **3. How difficulty grows:**
  - 1 · Glow (3–4): Sort by colour only
  - 2 · Flame (4–5): Sort by shape, then switch once to colour
  - 3 · Beacon (5–7): Switches every 3 cards
  - 4 · Lighthouse (7–9): Mixed: cards with a border use the other rule
- **4. How feedback teaches:** Right: the card settles into the basket. Old rule used: the moon pulses and Nova says "The moon says colour now." The card returns to the child's hand.
